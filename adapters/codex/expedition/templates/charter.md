# Charter: {expedition name}

The charter is drawn before departure and expires with the expedition. It names what the company seeks and the bounds within which the frontiersman acts freely — not every fork in the trail. Too narrow, and the company stops at every fork to wait for word. Too loose, and every direction appears authorized.

## Purpose

What the company seeks and why it matters. What must never be sacrificed.

## Destination

The intended destination, named as best it can be from home, expecting the nearer view to bring detail and correction.

## Arrival

What will count as arrival — how patron and frontiersman alike will know the expedition has accomplished its commission.

## First approach

The route the patron approves at departure. The frontiersman may depart from it when the country requires; within the charter, a flooded river needs no rider home.

## Provisions

What the expedition carries and may spend: time, budget, tools, access, dependencies.

## Dangers accepted in advance

Risks the patron accepts now so they need not be re-asked later.

## Authority

How far the frontiersman may act in the patron's name without sending word home.

## Riders

The questions that must ride home before acting. Some questions can only be answered where the bounds were drawn: another horizon, spending beyond provisions, dangers not agreed upon, a purpose out of reach.
