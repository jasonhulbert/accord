# Report: {task} — {date}

A report orients the human to the work. It keeps failure and uncertainty
visible, points to what can be inspected, and carries the agent's counsel. It
does not stand in for the work itself.

## What changed

Progress and material changes since the previous report.

## What did not work

Failed attempts, their cost, and what they revealed.

## What we know now

Evidence that changed or sharpened the understanding of the work, including
anything that changes its value, cost, or risk.

## Work to inspect

At a review, point to the actual work or evidence and give enough orientation
to judge it. Name the slice in view, the judgment sought, the choices still
open, and what will grow harder to change afterward.

## What comes next

The approach the agent recommends from here. At an open review, give counsel
without presuming the human's direction.

## Questions and counsel

Questions kept by the human, if any. State the agent's recommendation and the
evidence behind it.
